# Other manuscript sections

## Model and problem formulation

Introduce entities, states, inputs and dynamics, followed by available information, assumptions and the objective. Define variables near equations and preserve units and quantifiers.

`Consider a {system} with {entities} in {domain}. Let {symbol} denote {quantity}. The dynamics of {system} are given by {equation}. It is assumed that {condition}. The objective is to {task} subject to {constraints}.`

## Method

Explain the condition motivating each module before presenting its operation. Choose the actual architecture rather than imposing an observer, learning component or geometric construction on every paper.

`Based on {available information}, the {original problem} is reformulated as {new problem}. A {module} is developed to {specific purpose}. The {quantity} is obtained using {actual mechanism}.`

## Theory and proof

Match the statement type to its role: Assumption, Definition, Lemma, Proposition, Theorem or Corollary. No special personal Proposition wording is prescribed. State exact conditions and conclusions.

`Under {conditions}, {precise property} holds.`

Proof options include defining an auxiliary object, selecting an appropriate Lyapunov function, differentiating a specified relation, substituting established identities, and deriving the final property. Use `From {equations}, {deduction} follows` when justified. Finish by establishing the claimed result under its actual assumptions.

Preserve distinctions between necessary and sufficient, local and global, candidate and optimal, boundedness and convergence. Do not treat an observation property as a terminal-task guarantee.

## Results

Present the scenario or platform, parameters, implementation, figure-linked observations, controlled comparisons and bounded conclusion. Keep simulation, software-in-the-loop and physical experiments distinct.

`Fig. {number} depicts {observable}. Under {tested setting}, {measured finding} is observed. A comparative study is conducted using {baseline} with {controlled setup}.`

Use numerical values only when supplied. An empirical trajectory does not prove a universal theorem. Explain differences between theoretical assumptions and implementation conditions when relevant.

## Conclusion

Summarize the problem and conditions, the mechanism, the principal scoped result and actual validation. Include future work only when it is a real unresolved extension. Do not add an unsupported numerical gain, guarantee or generic future-work sentence to fill a template.
