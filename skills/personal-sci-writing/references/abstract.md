# Abstract patterns

Choose the sequence that fits the supplied work. The source collection supports variable-length Abstracts and both problem-first and method-first variants. A separate challenge sentence is optional. Do not enforce a fixed sentence count.

## Theory-oriented

Problem and conditions → central mathematical difficulty if needed → construction or analysis → scoped theoretical result → available validation.

`A {specific problem} under {conditions} is investigated. A {construction} is developed using {mechanism}. Under {assumptions}, {specific relation} is derived. The {property} is established for {exact domain}. The {validation type} demonstrates {supported finding}.`

Keep necessary/sufficient qualifiers, policy restrictions and boundary exclusions. Do not promote an inner approximation to an exact characterization.

## Method design

Problem and constraints → overall framework → component functions → actual guarantee → available validation.

`The {control problem} is addressed for {system} subject to {constraints}. A {framework} is proposed. The {observer} is designed to estimate {quantity}. A {controller} is developed using {available information}. Under {conditions}, {proved property} is guaranteed. Simulation results demonstrate {supported finding}.`

Use a proof sentence only if a proof exists. Long component lists can be split at genuine claim boundaries.

## Theory combined with learning

Task → framework → information/representation → problem reformulation → learning mechanism → precise theory → available validation.

`The {problem} is studied under {conditions}. A {learning framework} is constructed. The {information} is estimated using {observer}. The {original task} is reformulated as {new problem}. The {policies} are learned using {algorithm and data}. The {precisely proved property} is established under {assumptions}. Experimental results validate {measured capability}.`

A method-first alternative is useful when representation or architecture is the central contribution. Describe the method and task, its mechanism, the scoped property, and the actual validation. Do not invent a final validation sentence if no results are supplied.

## Verb strength

`investigated`, `studied` and `addressed` describe scope. `derived` describes a construction or relation. `proved`, `guaranteed` and `solved` require evidence at that strength. A theorem about an ideal mathematical object is not automatically a guarantee for its learned approximation. Simulation, software-in-the-loop and physical experiments are different forms of evidence.
